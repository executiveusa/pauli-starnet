# Pacing, voice and visuals

## Structure
- Open on the promised problem/result.
- Alternate claim, proof/example, implication, and next question.
- Use chapters only when they help orientation.
- Earn length through useful depth; do not stretch.
- End by resolving the promise and directing one next step.

## Voice
Direct, specific, analytical, evidence-led. Speak to a real viewer with a problem, not to "the algorithm." Use plain words. Separate proved results from estimates and hypotheses.

## Visuals
Each visual must prove, orient or reset attention. Favor source screens, diagrams, receipts, before/after states, demonstrations and concrete examples. Avoid decorative stock loops, unlicensed clips, duplicated scene templates and synthetic visuals that imply false evidence.

## Faceless adaptation
A faceless video still needs authorship: original research, a real point of view, a distinct narration treatment, rights-cleared visual logic, meaningful variation and an editorial pass.

## Evidence status
These production patterns come from creator frameworks and remain hypotheses until first-party tests or independent evidence support them. Never present a creator income, client-result, performance, or causal claim as verified merely because it appears in a transcript.
