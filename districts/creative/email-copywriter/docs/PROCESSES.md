# Alan Dragou email process cards

These are interpretations of retrieved captions, not a claim that every channel video was studied line by line. Each card cites the particular video(s) read for the method. His personal revenue and conversion figures are anecdotes, not substantiation for a client. Use actual source transcript files for a contested detail. Timestamps are retained in `.vtt` in the archive; `.txt` is cleaned reading text.

## 1. T-O-C: target, offer, copy
1. Identify a narrow audience with a real problem and desired change. Learn their words by being in the market, observing as a buyer, or talking to people in it.
2. Pick an offer that solves the actual problem, and collect honest proof and terms.
3. Only then shape the hook, story/argument, and CTA. Do not expect phrasing to repair a weak offer or wrong audience.
Source: `aGCi1L71oCc` [Modern Copywriting Full Course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGCi1L71oCc), especially its T-O-C and market-research sections.

## 2. Reader-language research
1. Observe the market directly, sample relevant public discussions and product reviews, and ask the actual client for sales/support conversations.
2. Record exact phrasing with source links, grouped by fears, frustrations, wants, beliefs, joys and objections.
3. Use AI to cluster evidence, not create synthetic quotes; open and check the linked originals before using any claim.
4. Choose a single problem and a credible mechanism to address it. Mark unknowns.
Sources: `aGCi1L71oCc` [full course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGCi1L71oCc); `7dQmjRjP6Fc` [Rapid Market Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7dQmjRjP6Fc). The latter specifically requests authentic quotes and source links from Reddit in an AI-assisted research demo.

## 3. SLO editorial email: story, lesson, offer
1. Find a true, permission-safe scene relevant to the reader.
2. Open in action, tell enough of the story to make the lesson understandable, then pivot cleanly to one insight.
3. Connect an offer that helps act on that lesson; one CTA. The Short says the story may take roughly 80% of the email, but treat that as the creator's example, not a universal quota.
4. Make the email worth reading even if no click occurs. Never make up a personal experience for a client.
Sources: `F1SIbxLJmpY` [This Email Framework](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F1SIbxLJmpY); `6GC7zCe_Nzw` [1,000 Emails Short](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6GC7zCe_Nzw); `jxDdVrMnWtM` [E-Commerce Emails](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jxDdVrMnWtM).

## 4. 5C subject-line ideation
1. Generate subject candidates using relevant **curiosity**, **crazy** (unusual true detail), **challenge** (a grounded contrary belief), **culture** (a reference the reader actually knows), or **credibility** (real proof or attributed authority).
2. Select a subject whose promise the body pays off; test variations on genuine reader segments.
3. Don't turn the "crazy" or credibility levers into clickbait, unsupported authority, or fabricated numbers.
Source: `AZ6J7PoLI5c` [Irresistible Email Subject Lines](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AZ6J7PoLI5c). The caption's final recap names all five Cs.

## 5. Story mechanics, not borrowed stories
1. Open with dialogue or visible action rather than generic setup.
2. Replace vague verbs and objects with true, concrete detail.
3. Establish stakes that the actual character or reader faces, and connect the scene to the offer only when relevant.
4. A problem -> setback -> earned change can carry a story, but a borrowed anecdote needs permission and verification.
Sources: `aliJVqNjbKY` [Master Email Storytelling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aliJVqNjbKY); `aoXUcMGmfmM` [Storytelling Frameworks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aoXUcMGmfmM); `Em_js8VsUmM` [Pixar Secrets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Em_js8VsUmM).

## 6. Opt-in before traffic
1. Measure visitor-to-subscriber conversion before pushing more traffic. State the exact reader and immediate problem.
2. Make the free resource (his term "bribe") congruent with the article/video or landing page that led there; promise a useful, specific outcome and deliver it.
3. On the opt-in page, combine audience, problem, desire and mechanism in the headline with actual evidence and an honest button. Test conversion by segment, not a borrowed benchmark.
Sources: `pOcHVQVhgFA` [Before You Grow an Email List](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pOcHVQVhgFA); `nQBe2ojQyqo` [Email Opt-In](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQBe2ojQyqo). Its title claims 68% for one example, not a transferable prediction.

## 7. Congruent gift-to-offer automation
1. Create an evergreen opt-in tied to a specific topic, then deliver the promised resource immediately in the first email.
2. If the reader consented to further messages, send a later, relevant next-step offer as a distinct email. The creator demonstrates a three-hour interval, which is a case example, not a default cadence.
3. Verify signup source, suppression, frequency, offer terms and unsubscribe behavior before any activation; test the path end-to-end.
Source: `zr0Eg4Kdg7g` [Email Automations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zr0Eg4Kdg7g).

## 8. Personality-led brand email / e-commerce campaign
1. Ask the brand for real behind-the-scenes moments (the creator suggests a short client voice note). Write from an approved person or honest brand voice, not a fictitious founder.
2. Map a campaign calendar with the job, subject, proposed date and approval state. Mix story, lesson and product context rather than a repetitive parade of discounts.
3. Test a simple text-forward mobile-readable format against the brand's existing design; don't claim it always improves deliverability.
4. Where useful, run connected stories over several emails, but respect list consent and fatigue rather than adopting a blanket high-frequency rule.
Sources: `jxDdVrMnWtM` [E-Commerce Emails](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jxDdVrMnWtM); `DWy5A5HezNU` [E-Commerce Mistakes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DWy5A5HezNU); `j6NuKWyl6sc` [New Rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6NuKWyl6sc).

## 9. Short launch sequence for a small problem
1. Collect recurring narrow problems from audience evidence. Make a useful free solution for one and a clearly related paid solution for a deeper adjacent problem.
2. Draft a short sequence explaining the problem, free win, product creation story, tangible benefits and actual offer. The creator illustrates a 3-5 day launch; don't schedule it automatically.
3. Ensure the free item really delivers; get approved prices/deadlines and test the links, terms and any optional order bumps before release.
Source: `ncvBs8NW8gY` [How to Make $10K in 3 Days](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ncvBs8NW8gY). The title's outcome is the creator's claim, not a forecast.

## 10. Revision and controlled tests
1. Reject audience-agnostic writing, vague headline, fake proof, product-first monologue, and stolen examples.
2. Draft 3-5 subjects or leads, compare them to actual reader language and proof, pick one, then test one meaningful lever at a time against a stated metric.
3. Keep the decision ledger: why this structure, what proof supports it, what result would change the next draft.
Sources: `8z_S6Fr_OSw` [Stop Writing Emails Like This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8z_S6Fr_OSw); `OCjiF8eUHPQ` [Frameworks Top Copywriters Use](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OCjiF8eUHPQ). The latter teaches AIDA (attention-interest-desire-action) and BAB (before-after-bridge), among standard frameworks; choose by the job, don't stack every formula.
