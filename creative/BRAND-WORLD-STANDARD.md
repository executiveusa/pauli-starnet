# Brand World Standard

Permanent law:

> **Preserve standards. Forget style. Every brand earns its own world.**

This standard belongs to the Creative District. It governs unrelated client/product visual identity before visual BUILD begins.

## What may persist globally

Preserve quality standards across projects:

- usability and low cognitive friction;
- ADHD-friendly clarity;
- accessibility;
- craftsmanship and reduction;
- responsive behavior;
- Steve Krug clarity principles;
- Collins-level rigor;
- Apple-level polish;
- Awwwards-level ambition when appropriate;
- Impeccable / anti-slop editing;
- performance;
- verification;
- truthful telemetry;
- strong hierarchy;
- human control and recoverability.

These are quality mechanisms, not a house aesthetic.

## What must not persist globally

Do not treat a successful prior project's appearance as a default.

Do not globally default to:

- white or cream backgrounds;
- editorial minimalism;
- oversized whitespace;
- giant headlines;
- serif/sans pairings;
- Inter, Geist, or Space Grotesk;
- split heroes;
- muted palettes;
- thin rules;
- rounded cards;
- magazine grids;
- bento grids;
- glassmorphism;
- generic centered SaaS heroes;
- recurring motion, image, component, or layout patterns from unrelated projects.

Reuse engineering behavior freely. Do not reuse unrelated brand appearance.

## Required Brand World Contract

Before visual BUILD, every unrelated project must define:

1. **Visual World** — the place, medium, material, environment, or cultural world the brand inhabits.
2. **Emotional Target** — what the user should feel and why that supports the product outcome.
3. **Cultural Sources** — relevant geography, communities, history, industry, artifacts, materials, rituals, subcultures, and references.
4. **Typography** — type behavior, provenance, personality, rhythm, and project-specific rationale.
5. **Color Behavior** — contrast, saturation, temperature, surface logic, signaling, and environmental use.
6. **Density** — sparse, compact, layered, immersive, utilitarian, expressive, or another justified level.
7. **Composition** — grid, rhythm, alignment, framing, asymmetry/symmetry, depth, and spatial behavior.
8. **Image Language** — photography, documentary, UGC, archival, illustration, 3D, collage, texture, diagrams, or another intentional system.
9. **Motion Language** — pacing, choreography, physical metaphor, transitions, interaction feedback, and reduced-motion behavior.
10. **Anti-References** — explicit portfolio projects, visual territories, trends, and model defaults this project must not resemble.

The contract must be specific enough that a reviewer can distinguish the project from the rest of the portfolio without reading its logo or copy.

## Derivation rule

A project's visual identity must derive from its own:

- audience;
- culture;
- geography;
- industry;
- history;
- brand personality;
- product behavior;
- emotional target;
- imagery;
- competitive context;
- physical/material context;
- trust requirements.

Consistency belongs inside a brand, not across unrelated brands.

## ADHD-friendly rule

ADHD-friendly means low cognitive friction:

- clear priorities;
- visible next actions;
- understandable states;
- strong information scent;
- progressive disclosure;
- recoverability;
- reduced working-memory burden.

It does not require minimalism, pale surfaces, large whitespace, or low visual energy.

## Benchmark rule

COLLINS, Apple, Awwwards, Impeccable, Steve Krug, MaxFusion, TryItNow, and similar references are quality or project-specific benchmarks, never global visual templates.

Extract principles and execution quality. Do not inherit surface identity unless the current project independently justifies it.

## Portfolio collision gate

Before SHIP, compare the design against relevant existing GitHub / Netlify / Vercel portfolio work.

Ask:

- Does it resemble another project before reading the copy?
- Would swapping logo, copy, color, and imagery make the two designs interchangeable?
- Are typography, hero composition, card language, spacing rhythm, image treatment, or motion recurring without project-specific reason?
- Is this a prior house aesthetic wearing different content?

If yes, verdict = **HOLD**. Return to the Brand World Contract and redesign the colliding decisions.

A new logo, palette, or image set does not make a duplicated composition a new brand.

## Scope boundary

This standard does not replace:

- project-specific design briefs;
- project-specific design specs;
- landing structural audits;
- accessibility checks;
- performance checks;
- the existing Gauntlet Loop;
- Perfectionist / Product Perfect QA;
- owner approval.

It adds one missing responsibility: prove that an unrelated brand earned its own visual world.

## Separation of concerns

```text
StarNet's own UI identity
        !=
global product-quality standards
        !=
client/product brand identity
```

The StarNet app may keep its own CRT / station design language. That identity must not become the visual default for unrelated client products.

## Final law

> **Preserve standards. Forget style. Every brand earns its own world.**
