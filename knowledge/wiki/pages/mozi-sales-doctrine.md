# Mozi sales doctrine (compiled)

Compiled from the 33-node Hormozi knowledge graph (`mozi-sales`, 43 transcripts). One home per node: each entry sits under its first workflow stage and names the others it applies to. Verbatim evidence stays in the graph; this page is the interpretation layer. Source home: `districts/sales/mozi/`. Related: [[overview]].

## Diagnose

- **Fix the business back to front** (`back-to-front-fix`, framework) - Diagnose and fix in this order: (1) the thing you sell (offer), (2) how you sell it (conversion/sales process), (3) who you sell to and how they find out (traffic). Never start with traffic. Mozi runs every engagement as this sequence. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M, zzleYxkf39k.

## Pain research

- **Pain and problem before product (house rule)** (`pain-first-research`, principle) - House rule aligning Mozi to the landing-page standard: before any offer or pitch work, mine the buyer's real pain in their own words (Reddit/forum verbatims, DM/FAQ logs, reviews). Every product is different; the workflow is the same: understand the pain and problem first, then apply the Hormozi spine. Hooks and first lines pressure-test against those verbatims. Also: diagnose, call prep. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M, house:landing-page-standard.

## Offer design

- **Value variables: speed, risk, ease** (`offer-value-variables`, framework) - Differentiation is built from value variables - speed, risk, ease (the operational face of the value equation: dream outcome, perceived likelihood, time delay, effort/sacrifice). When a product looks commoditized, add components that move these variables, not discounts. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
- **Break the commodity frame or die on price** (`break-commodity-frame`, doctrine) - If the buyer compares you on one measured unit (hourly rate), you are a commodity. Reframe the unit (per job, per outcome) and load the conversation with other variables, because buyers default to price only when given nothing else to evaluate. Also: pitch, objection handling. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
- **Invent internal certification levels** (`internal-certification-levels`, pattern) - Turn undifferentiated labor into tiered perceived expertise with internal certification levels (Level 3 master mover = 50+ moves; Level 3 trainer = 1,000 sessions). Converts a risk variable into a named, sellable asset at near-zero cost. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
- **The price cap (risk reversal)** (`price-cap-risk-reversal`, pattern) - Offer a cost ceiling ('it will not be more than this') to reverse the buyer's open-ended-meter fear, then use it as a seed of doubt against competitors who won't cap. Also: objection handling. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
- **Charge for speed, keep raising until they say no** (`speed-premium`, doctrine) - Speed is one of the biggest things people pay for. Add a same-day/expedite surcharge (start 10%, move toward 20%+) and keep raising prices until the market objects. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M, zzleYxkf39k.
- **The false offer (opt-out inclusion)** (`false-offer`, pattern) - Include a high-perceived-value component most buyers will decline (e.g. unpacking). It inflates offer value at near-zero delivery cost because you know customer behavior better than they do. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
- **Charge extra for the high-touch path** (`checkout-convenience-premium`, pattern) - Self-serve checkout at base price; talk-to-a-human path costs more (e.g. +$500 for a 1:1). Monetizes the founder's time, pushes buyers to the scalable path, and prices the exception honestly. Also: close. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **Match billing shape to value shape** (`money-models-billing-match`, principle) - Education is not recurring revenue; services and consumables are. Bill high-value one-time components upfront; bill lower-value consumable backends recurring. Never bill flat monthly for value that decays after delivery. Feeds offer architecture and the 'sell the vacation' pillar design. Also: retention ascension. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos offer-lane:money-models-extraction.
- **Price complaints: tier down only for the ideal buyer** (`price-tier-evidence`, doctrine) - If IDEAL customers say it is too expensive, add a cheaper tier (Skool went $99->$9 for hobbyists, 11x cheaper). If non-ideal people complain, the price is doing its job as a filter - ignore it. Also: objection handling, retention ascension. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos -j8_YCWZ05Q.

## Call prep

- **People only feel sold to when it's done badly** (`good-selling-feels-good`, belief_breaker) - Use on founders/agents who resist selling. Every purchase they made happily was engineered by a seller; good structure is service, not pressure. Blocking sales structure blocks the mission. Also: discovery. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **VSL before every sales conversation** (`vsl-before-call`, pattern) - Send a video sales letter before every sales conversation type (one per conversation: first call, renewal, event rebook). The VSL pre-frames the call and pre-handles that conversation's specific objections; the call then starts mid-funnel. Also: pitch. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos mRlSb0O5QNU.
- **CLOSER call framework** (`closer-framework`, framework) - The sales-call spine: C - Clarify why the person is there (label current state, desired state, the gap; position yourself as the bridge). L - Label them with a problem. O - Overview past pain: what they tried; associate with what they liked, disassociate from what they hated; loop 'what else' (the pain cycle). S - Sell the vacation: pitch as three pillars (any offer chunks up or down to three). E - Explain away concerns: people blame circumstances, other people, or avoid deciding; answer, then close. R - Reinforce the decision: after the yes, make next steps clear and keep those exact promises on time - the sale-to-onboarding handoff. Also: discovery, pitch, objection handling, close, reinforce onboard. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos shorts:ZMW8AfQeGqQ.

## Discovery

- **The 'what else' pain loop** (`pain-cycle-loop`, doctrine) - In discovery, keep asking 'what else' until the buyer has voiced the full pain inventory; associate your mechanism with what they liked in past attempts, disassociate from what they hated. Unheard pain becomes mid-pitch objections. Also: pain research. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos shorts:ZMW8AfQeGqQ.

## Pitch

- **Seeds of doubt against the competition** (`seeds-of-doubt`, doctrine) - Win commodity comparisons by surfacing the competitor's hidden costs and risks the buyer didn't think to ask about (nickel-and-diming, uninsured labor, no price cap, day-labor hires). Frame as questions, not attacks: 'Did the other guys give you a cap?' Also: objection handling. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
- **Present the top tier first, walk down** (`start-high-tiering`, doctrine) - Always present the premium tier first; buyers with money 'just want it done' and need the opportunity to buy at the top. On a budget objection, walk down to standard - never start low and try to walk up. Also: close. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
- **Structured pitch architecture (webinar/VSL/presentation)** (`structured-pitch-architecture`, framework) - Any persuasive presentation follows: (1) big promise - why stay; (2) who am I / origin story as old-way vs new-way transformation; (3) the 3 biggest problems, each handled as what-they-think > why-it's-wrong > what's-right > proof (stats, stories, as many forms as possible); (4) the offer; (5) FAQ by frequency. Structure raises value-per-second; it is clear communication, not 'bro marketing'. Also: call prep. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **Sell like everyone is cold** (`sell-to-cold`, doctrine) - Build every pitch as if the audience does not know you. Pitches built for cold audiences convert warm ones too; pitches built for warm audiences convert only warm ones and cap scale. Also: traffic. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **FAQ by frequency, answered live** (`faq-by-frequency`, pattern) - Mine the real question log (DMs, calls, inbox), bucket the questions, and answer them in order of greatest percentage - 40% question first, then 25%, then the long tail, live, for as long as people stay. There are no new questions. Also: objection handling, call prep. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **Every offer pitches as three pillars** (`three-pillar-pitch`, pattern) - Chunk any offer into exactly three pillars (fitness example: fitness, nutrition, accountability; realtor-leads example: timely, exclusive, qualified). Three is the memorable, sellable unit. Also: offer design. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos shorts:ZMW8AfQeGqQ.

## Close

- **Guarantee framed at the hidden objector** (`guarantee-reframe`, pattern) - Give a short no-sweat guarantee (e.g. 3 days) and aim it at the decision-blocker who is not in the room (the spouse). The guarantee language should name the absurd version of the objection, not just state terms. Also: objection handling. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **Never present an invoice by email** (`never-email-invoice`, rule) - Price and proposals are always presented live (phone/call) so objections surface and get handled in the moment. Emailed pricing kills deals silently. Also: objection handling. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos mRlSb0O5QNU.

## Reinforce onboard

- **Reinforce the decision after the yes** (`closer-reinforce`, doctrine) - The sale is not done at payment. Immediately make next steps explicit and keep those exact promises on time; the post-sale window is where refunds and churn are decided. This is the sale-to-onboarding handoff. Also: retention ascension. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos shorts:ZMW8AfQeGqQ.
- **Front-load the fast win; design busy path + power path** (`front-load-the-win`, pattern) - Move the stickiest thing (the long ladder) into onboarding; pin a fast win in the first 24 hours; remove snakes (friction, confusion, overwhelm). Design two explicit paths: a 'start here / weekly cadence' busy-member path and optional depth for power users. Owner shows up daily - communities with daily-active owners retain measurably higher. Also: retention ascension. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos -j8_YCWZ05Q.

## Retention ascension

- **Sell during the delivery, not after** (`sell-during-delivery`, doctrine) - Make the upsell/renewal asks at the halfway point, the two-thirds point, and the end - while the buyer is mid-decision and still hot. Selling only at the end converts about a third; selling during converts half or more. More asks = more takes. Also: close. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **Opt-out renewal, not opt-in** (`opt-out-billing`, pattern) - Structure renewals so the customer continues by default and must actively cancel (biannual auto-continuation), instead of requiring a new purchase decision every cycle. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos 8VqSFBMMS4M.
- **Churn as leaky bucket: joins vs cancels, benchmarks** (`churn-leaky-bucket`, framework) - Track joins vs cancels monthly and label the business growing/flat/declining. Skool platform benchmarks: 80% monthly retention is average, 90%+ is best, under 70% means retention is the highest-ROI lever. Moving retention 80->90% doubles LTV - small churn deltas are huge LTV swings. Also: diagnose. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos -j8_YCWZ05Q.
- **Churn collapses with tenure - engineer day-90 and month-6** (`churn-drops-with-tenure`, doctrine) - Months 1-3 churn 20%+; past day 90 it halves to ~10%; by month 6 it drops to ~2% (5x LTV). So the tactical question is never 'lower churn' but 'what gets them past day 90' (activation, onboarding, expectation setting, honest front-end promises) and 'what gets them past month 6' (delivered outcome + connection to other members). Never overhaul the business off month-1 churn - it is structurally the highest. Also: reinforce onboard. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos -j8_YCWZ05Q.
- **Ask why they canceled, fix patterns not anecdotes** (`cancel-reason-loop`, pattern) - Message every canceler, tag the reason (price, overwhelm, not using, missing feature, not what I expected), and every ~20 cancels fix the top 1-2 reasons. Keep a tight loop between support complaints and what gets built. Also: diagnose. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos -j8_YCWZ05Q.
- **Ask your best members why they stay - then subtract** (`ask-why-they-stay`, pattern) - Ask the longest-tenured, most-engaged customers what the number one value is; whatever repeats becomes the retention engine and everything else is optional. Fix retention by subtraction and friction-removal around the original promise, not by adding features. Also: offer design. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos -j8_YCWZ05Q.
- **Overwhelm is the #1 churn driver - more stuff is more reasons to cancel** (`overwhelm-kills-retention`, doctrine) - Outside price, overwhelm ('I can't keep up') drives churn. Five features where the customer uses one feels like 20% value; one feature feels like 100%. Planet Fitness won by stripping to the 10-15% people actually used. A Skool community DELETED content and cut churn from 30% to 5% (6x LTV). Sell value per second, not seconds of value. Also: offer design. Evidence: 2 quotes, videos -j8_YCWZ05Q.

## Referral partners

- **Pay referral partners in status, not cash** (`vip-partner-incentive`, pattern) - Referral partners (realtors, past clients) value making their own clients happy more than small kickbacks. Give referred buyers VIP-tier service at standard price instead of a discount or cash - it makes the referrer look good and costs little. Also: offer design. Evidence: 1 quotes, videos zzleYxkf39k.
